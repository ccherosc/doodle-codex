The whole art library for Norfair Chronicles, a real-time overhead action RPG with a world map for travel and local scenes for play. Everything is one pencil-doodle hand on a 64×64 grid. Every sprite below is the shipped PNG strip, played at its native 10 fps loop. The last section lists what is not drawn yet.

Terrain

20 terrains, 88 variants

Square, opaque, seamless 64×64 ground, six frames at 10 fps. Each base is shown 3×3 to prove the seam. A variant is its base plus one interior feature, and tiles against everything its base does; an engine picks one by a weighted hash on all-one-terrain cells. Listed low to high in draw order: the higher terrain owns the edge between two.

Transitions

19 overlay, 19 shore, 18 scorch, 6 cliff atlases

Borders are a corner-Wang set drawn on a dual grid: 16 masks per terrain, six frames each. A cell with several terrains is the lowest as a base plus one overlay per higher terrain. Shore atlases replace the overlay when the lowest terrain is water (foam), scorch when it is lava (charred crust). Diagonal roads, rivers and trails stay connected by design. Cliff atlases draw a height step inside one terrain (grass over grass) from an integer height per cell: a bank on the south-facing edge, a rim and shadow elsewhere, no ramps. Every overlay, shore and scorch atlas also has a second-seed alternate that can be mixed in tile by tile without a seam. The pictures below are static proofs built from the shipped atlases.

Wall kits

5 materials x 47 pieces, 24 door and gate pieces

Hand-placeable walls on the ordinary grid: one still piece per cell, and the cell is what it blocks. The name says where the piece sits in a wall; a 256-entry lookup on the eight neighbours picks it automatically. Walls do not animate. Kinds: interior (face 36 px); rock (face 24 px); stone (face 30 px); stone_low (face 12 px); timber (face 28 px). A door or gate replaces one straight piece of a run and ships closed and open.

Structures and props

51 props, 13 at a person's scale

Cut-outs that stand on terrain, drawn in elevation with a ground shadow. map scale means a landmark for the world map (a castle two tiles wide); local scale means drawn to stand beside a person. Trees block only the trunk, so a person can walk behind the crown. Some have a second state (open, raided).

Monsters

30 creatures, 26 elites, 198 states x 4 facings

UI and portraits

7 nine-slice pieces, 3 bar fills, 2 cursors, 2 fonts, portraits for 12 bodies and 4 lords

Parchment UI as nine-slice pieces (corners fixed, edges tiled), inventory slots, bars whose fill tiles, cursors, and a handwritten bitmap font in two sizes and two pencils (ink and chalk): every word in the game is written by the hand that drew it. Dialogue portraits are layered from the same wardrobe as the sprite, so a portrait always matches what the person is wearing, and carry the face detail a 64 px sprite cannot: irises, lids, nose, mouth, wrinkles, freckles, scars. Portraits are stills, facing the viewer.

Known gaps

what is not drawn yet
  1. Local scenes are still thin. Two houses, an inn, six pieces of furniture, two floors and five wall kits. No stairs, windows as pieces, rugs, signs, market stalls or street lamps at a person's scale; kinds of wall do not join each other.
  2. Cliffs. A height step has a drawn bank on its south-facing edge only; north, west and east edges are a rim and a shadow. No ramp or stair art, no foam where a cliff stands in water, and only six terrains have one.
  3. Landmarks. One design and no state each (no ruined, raided or lit variant) for the great landmarks.
  4. Monsters. The regional monsters have no signature state. The four lords have no elite. Monster portraits exist for the lords only.
  5. Portraits. Stills, facing the viewer, one expression per body. No talking or blinking frames.
  6. UI. No icons for status effects, no minimap frame, no scroll bar, no second language: the font covers ASCII 32-126 only.
  7. Faces. Sprites keep two eyes and a brow at 64 px by design; all face detail is in the portraits.
  8. Terrain. Farmland runs east-west or north-south, not diagonally. Alternates change 7 to 29 percent of an edge tile: they break a rhythm, they do not hide it.
  9. Gameplay data. Only suggested data ships: walkability per terrain, blocked cells, idle hurtboxes, impact frames. No per-state hitboxes.
